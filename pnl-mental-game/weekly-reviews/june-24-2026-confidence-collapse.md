# June 24, 2026 — Confidence Collapse Case Study

## Snapshot

- Closed P/L: -114.79
- Closed transactions: 16

## Technical Recognition

The June 24 chart study showed:

GOOD WHERE + GOOD WHEN + MEDIOCRE WHAT = NO SETUP.

The original NAS100 and SP500 ideas were not clean red-car setups at the decision point.

The technical mistake was allowing WHERE and WHEN to overpower WHAT.

Do not infer setup validity from the eventual P/L.

## Execution Sequence

The initial NAS buys lost approximately -50.22 combined.

After that early loss, the process became unstable.

The visible sequence developed into:

NAS BUY
-> SP500 SELL
-> SP500 BUY
-> SP500 SELL
-> SP500 BUY
-> return to NAS

The important issue was not simply the number of transactions.

The directional thesis itself kept changing.

## First Process Break

The first major break came after the initial NAS loss.

Instead of returning to a fresh WHEN / WHERE / WHAT decision, confidence collapsed and the next decisions became increasingly reactive.

The review should focus on that first break rather than treating every later ticket as an independent mistake.

## Mental Loop

The recurring loop was:

1. Recognize / enter
2. Loss or opposing movement appears
3. Confidence drops
4. Exit
5. Search for another answer
6. Switch market or direction
7. New loss increases emotional pressure
8. Flip again
9. Eventually return to the original market

This is confidence collapse / emotional switching.

## Multiple Tickets Distinction

Multiple tickets are not automatically overtrading.

They can express one planned recognition decision with size.

June 24 was different because the direction and market kept changing under pressure.

## Carry-Forward Rules

1. Good WHERE + good WHEN cannot rescue mediocre WHAT.
2. After a loss, reset with WHEN / WHERE / WHAT before any new decision.
3. A new market requires a fresh valid setup.
4. A direction flip requires an actual new valid template, not discomfort.
5. Do not let the previous loss choose the next trade.
6. Identify the FIRST confidence break before counting later entries.
