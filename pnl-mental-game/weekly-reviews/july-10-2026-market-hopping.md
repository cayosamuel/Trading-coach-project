The important feature is not simply the number of trades.

The day shows repeated movement between markets and, in some cases, changes in directional thesis.

## What the Day Shows

This is a market-hopping / thesis-instability example.

When one trade becomes uncomfortable or fails, attention can shift to another market before the original decision has been properly reviewed.

That can create a chain where each new trade is partly influenced by the emotional residue of the previous one.

## First Process Break

The review should identify the first point where the process stopped being:

WHEN
-> WHERE
-> WHAT
-> execute one clear thesis

and became reactive switching.

Everything after that first break may simply be downstream damage.

## What Not to Conclude

Do not conclude that every setup taken that day was technically invalid merely because the day lost money.

P/L does not determine setup validity.

Technical recognition must be reviewed separately from execution behavior.

## Carry-Forward Rules

1. A loss does not give permission to immediately search the basket for another trade.
2. A new market requires a fresh WHEN / WHERE / WHAT decision.
3. A direction flip requires an actual new valid setup.
4. Do not let discomfort in one market become the reason for entering another.
5. After an exit, identify whether the original thesis was truly invalidated before switching.
6. Review the FIRST process break instead of treating every later trade as unrelated.
